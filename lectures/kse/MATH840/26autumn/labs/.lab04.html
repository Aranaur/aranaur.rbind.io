🎯 The task

This is the first of five challenges. From today you have your own time series, and one number to beat on it.

Forecast the next \(h\) observations of your series and beat your benchmark — with the toolbox from Week 3 and nothing more. No ETS, no ARIMA: those are the next challenges, and this one is the measurement that makes their improvement visible.

🗂 Your series

Your series is issued by a hash of the same identifier you have used since Week 1. The template does it for you: it turns your identifier into a code such as TS-4DD7A and downloads that series.

NoteWhat “anonymised” means here

The series is real. What has been removed is its identity: no name, no units, values rescaled, the calendar shifted by whole years, the history trimmed. Two students never get the same window of the same series, so a forecast is worth exactly what your model is worth.

You are not expected to work out what it measures, and you will not be marked for trying. Read the data, not the domain.

This series is yours for the rest of the course. Challenges 2–5 (ETS, ARIMA, seasonal ARIMA, advanced methods) run on the same series, so you will watch your own forecast improve — or fail to.

📐 Section 2 — Forecast and accuracy (3 points)

Your benchmark

The benchmark is the best of the four simple methods from Week 1 — mean, naive, snaive, drift — chosen on a validation window: the last \(h\) observations of the history you were given.

Nothing about it is hidden. The template computes it, and the scorer computes it the same way, from the same public data:

  1. fit the four methods to everything except the last \(h\) observations;
  2. score them on those \(h\) observations by RMSE, and keep the winner;
  3. refit the winner on the whole history you were given — that is your benchmark.

How the points are awarded

Your forecast and the benchmark’s are both scored by MASE on the \(h\) observations that come after your history, which you have not seen.

\[ \text{skill} = \frac{\text{MASE}_{\text{you}}}{\text{MASE}_{\text{benchmark}}} \]

Result Points
skill \(< 1.00\) — you beat the benchmark 3
skill \(\le 1.10\) — within 10% of it, or equal to it 2
worse than that 1, and Section 3 must say why
NoteYour series is known to be beatable

Every series in the pool was screened: it is only issued if a reference model lands between 0.70 and 0.90 of its benchmark. Below that the series is trivial, above it effectively unbeatable. If you do not beat your benchmark, that is a fact about your model, not your luck.

The benchmark is also checked for being a fair target: series where one of the other three simple methods would have beaten the chosen one by more than 15% on the hidden holdout were thrown out, so you are not competing against an unlucky pick.

The screening reference was an automatic ETS or ARIMA — models you meet in the next two challenges. With the toolbox alone, matching the benchmark is a solid result and beating it is a good one.

What the toolbox gives you

Everything in this list is Week 3 material, and each of them can beat a simple method:

  • a transformation — Box-Cox, if the seasonal swing grows with the level;
  • forecasting with decomposition: STL the series, forecast the seasonally adjusted part with naive or drift, carry the seasonal component forward with snaive, add them back;
  • a combination: the average of two or three forecasts is often better than any of them;
  • residual diagnostics to catch a model that is leaving structure behind.

✍️ Section 3 — Justification block (3 points)

Three points, and the only part of the work a human reads closely. Four things, roughly a paragraph each:

Item Points What earns them
3.1 What the data shows 0.75 Frequency, seasonal period, trend, level shifts, outliers — with the plot that shows each claim
3.2 Why this method 0.75 The method you submitted and why it fits what you just described, including what you tried and dropped
3.3 What the residuals say 0.75 ACF and a Ljung-Box test on your final model’s residuals, read — what structure is left, and whether it matters at this horizon
3.4 What you expect 0.75 Your own MASE on the validation window against the benchmark’s, and the number you expect on the hidden holdout
Important

Marked on the reasoning, not on the outcome. A well-argued forecast that loses to the benchmark scores full marks in Section 3 — and if it loses, say what you think the holdout contained that your validation window did not.

🧹 Section 4 — Code quality and reproducibility (1 point)

  • The notebook runs top to bottom on a fresh kernel without errors.
  • Your submission file is written by the notebook, not edited by hand.
  • No leakage: nothing after the end of your history exists, so nothing can be fitted to it. If you tune on the validation window, say so in 3.2 — that is legitimate, and it is also why your validation MASE is optimistic.
