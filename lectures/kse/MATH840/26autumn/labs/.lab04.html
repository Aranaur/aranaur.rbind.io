📐 Section 2 — Decomposition and forecast (3 points)

Item Points What earns them
2.1 1 The decomposition. STL with period equal to your series’ seasonal period, components plotted, and the seasonally adjusted series computed from it. A decomposition with the wrong period earns nothing here, and nothing after it.
2.2 1 The forecast, built from the parts. The seasonally adjusted series forecast with a simple method, the seasonal component carried forward, the two added back. Candidates compared on the validation window — the last \(h\) observations — with MASE, against all four simple methods.
2.3 1 The submission. Your chosen approach refit on the whole history, \(h\) steps ahead, with an 80% interval, written to the submission file by the notebook.
WarningWhere this goes wrong
  • The seasonal period is M from the setup cell — 12 for monthly series, 4 for quarterly. Not the number of years, not 7.
  • Forecast the adjusted series, not the raw one, and add the season back afterwards. Doing one without the other is the most common way to lose both marks in 2.2.
  • STL needs seasonal to be an odd integer ≥ 3, and an int.
  • Refit on the whole history before forecasting the hidden horizon. Forecasting from the training part only throws away your last \(h\) observations — the most informative ones you have.

✍️ Section 3 — Justification block (3 points)

Three points, and the part a human reads closely. Roughly a paragraph each, with numbers from your own cells rather than adjectives.

Item Points What earns them
3.1 0.75 What the data shows. Frequency, seasonal period, trend, level shifts, outliers — each claim tied to a plot from Section 1.
3.2 0.75 Why this decomposition and this level method. Your choice of seasonal and robust, and of the method that forecasts the adjusted series, tied to what you just described. Say what you tried and dropped.
3.3 0.75 What the residuals say. ACF and a Ljung-Box test on your final model’s residuals, read: what structure is left, and what it damages — the point forecast or the interval.
3.4 0.75 What you expect. Your MASE on the validation window against all four simple methods, and what you expect on the hidden horizon. If you expect to lose to a simple method, say which and why.
Important

Marked on the reasoning. A decomposition forecast that loses to seasonal naive and explains exactly why scores full marks here; one that wins and cannot say why does not.

🧹 Section 4 — Code quality and reproducibility (1 point)
