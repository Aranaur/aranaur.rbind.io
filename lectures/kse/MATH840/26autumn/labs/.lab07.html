🎯 The task

Forecast your series from Week 4 with ARIMA, as the ARIMA Models, Part 1 lecture defines it:

  • differencing, including one seasonal difference if the tests ask for it;
  • a non-seasonal ARMA(\(p\), \(q\)) on the differenced series;
  • optionally a log transformation, if the seasonal swing grows with the level;
  • no seasonal AR or MA terms: \(P = Q = 0\). Those are the Part 2 lecture and Lab 7.

The order matters, and it is marked: difference, read, specify by hand — and only then compare with AutoARIMA.

No ETS, no regression, no averaging with other methods.

So this week the points go to the differencing, the specification and the argument. The score is still published: it is the number Lab 7, with seasonal terms, will be compared against.

📊 Section 1 — EDA and visualisation (1 point)

You know this series by now. The plots this week have one job: to justify the differencing and the transformation before any model is fitted.

  • A time plot, read for trend, season, and whether the seasonal swing grows with the level. If it grows, that is your case for log=True — ARIMA has no multiplicative season, and the log is how it gets one.
  • The validation split on a plot: history, validation window, your chosen model’s forecast and 80% interval over it.
  • One sentence per plot. A plot with no reading attached earns nothing.

📐 Section 2 — Model and forecast (3 points)

Item Points What earns them
2.1 1 Differencing. difference_plan with M from the setup cell, on \(y\) or \(\log y\) as Section 1 argued; the differenced series plotted. Overriding a test is allowed and must be argued in 3.1.
2.2 1 Specification and comparison. ACF and PACF of the differenced series out to lag \(3m\); at least two hand-specified candidates written down from that reading; AutoARIMA added; all compared with compare and decided with rolling_mase, the benchmark in the same table.
2.3 1 The submission. The chosen candidate refitted on the whole history, \(h\) steps, with the model’s 80% interval, written to the file by the notebook.
WarningWhere this goes wrong
  • Fitting outside fit_arima. It is the only builder in the template, and it refuses seasonal AR and MA terms. AutoARIMA(season_length=M) on its own will add them — that model is Lab 7’s.
  • Comparing AICc across different \(d\), \(D\) or log choices. AICc is comparable only between models fitted to the same differenced data. Across those choices, rolling_mase decides.
  • Ljung-Box with model_df=0. The template’s compare uses \(p + q\) — use the same if you test by hand.
  • Reading the residual spike at lag \(m\) as a mistake. With \(P = Q = 0\) it is often expected: the seasonal difference over-corrects. Report it in 3.3; Lab 7 is where it gets fixed.
  • Forecasting from train. The submission is refitted on the whole history.

✍️ Section 3 — Justification block (3 points)

The part a human reads closely. Roughly a paragraph each, with numbers from your own cells.

Item Points What earns them
3.1 1 The differencing. \(D\), \(d\) and the log decision, each tied to a test or a plot. If you overrode a test, why — and what the ACF of the result looked like.
3.2 1 The hand reading and the machine. The lags in the ACF and PACF that named your candidates; what AutoARIMA chose and why it agrees or disagrees with you; the candidate you submitted and the rolling-origin evidence for it.
3.3 1 What is left, and what you expect. Ljung-Box with \(p + q\) degrees of freedom, read; the residual autocorrelation at lag \(m\) and what it says about \(P = Q = 0\); your expected skill against the benchmark, and how this model compares with your Lab 5 ETS on the validation window.
Important

Marked on the reasoning. A hand specification that AutoARIMA beats, with a clear account of why, scores full marks. So does a series where the honest answer is a random walk — if the ACF of the differences is what you show for it.
