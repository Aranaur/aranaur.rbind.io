  • The pool was screened so that AutoETS and AutoARIMA each beat the benchmark — so every row with them is flattered by construction. MSTL, the networks and the foundation models were not screened: their rows are what you would expect on fresh data.
  • A network trained on one series is close to a coin flip against the benchmark: a few hundred observations are little to learn from.
  • A foundation model alone beats the benchmark on 76–84% of the series and can lose badly on the rest. Added to the old models it gave the best median of all — the Week 9 lesson again.
  • Intervals of AutoETS + AutoARIMA + MSTL, on the hidden horizon: the averaged bounds cover 78% of the points on average, the conformal recipe 79%; Winkler scores 3.75 and 3.85. Neither recipe wins in general — which is why you choose on your own windows.
  • Your Lab 5 and Lab 7 models may not be the automatic ones. The table is a guide, not a promise.

WarningWhere this goes wrong
  • Choosing on the validation window alone. Four windows, and the scoreboard’s mean — not window 1.
  • Choosing the members or the mix after reading the table. Then it is a selection in disguise. Write the notes in 2.0 and 2.1 first.
  • Reading one seed as the network’s score. A small network on one short series can move a lot from seed to seed. Run your best one twice.
  • “New” read as “better”. On many Track B series ETS or ARIMA is still the one to beat.
  • Judging an interval on the windows it was built from. interval_check leaves each window out.
  • Forecasting from train. final_forecast calls every forecaster again on the whole of y.
  • An API key in the notebook. TimeGPT is welcome in track D; the key comes from a Colab secret.
