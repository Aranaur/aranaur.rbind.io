🎯 The task

Forecast your series from Week 4 with seasonal ARIMA, \(\text{ARIMA}(p,d,q)(P,D,Q)_m\) — the ARIMA Models, Part 2 lecture. The order matters, and it is marked:

  1. Difference — \(D\) and \(d\) from the tests, \(\log y\) if the seasonal swing grows with the level.
  2. Read the ACF and PACF of the differenced series: \(p, q\) at lags \(1, 2, 3\); \(P, Q\) at lags \(m, 2m, 3m\).
  3. Specify by hand at least two candidates — then add AutoARIMA.
  4. Decide on a rolling origin, check the residuals with \(p + q + P + Q\), submit.

ARIMA only: no ETS, no regression, no averaging with other methods. Combining is next week’s lecture.

NoteWhat the pool guarantees, and what it does not

Every series in the pool was issued only if AutoARIMA with season_length=m, fitted on the whole raw history, beats its benchmark on the hidden horizon. That is a guarantee about that one model: not about every seasonal ARIMA, not about AutoARIMA on \(\log y\), and not about the orders AutoARIMA found on train.

Measured on the whole pool with this template: choosing among the airline model and AutoARIMA, each with and without a log, by rolling_mase, beats the benchmark on 89% of series, comes within 10% on another 9%, and loses on 2%. AutoARIMA on the raw history alone beats it on all 140 — the screen guarantees that.

Hand-specified models and logs are what the rubric asks you to try — follow your evidence, and say in 3.2 what it was.

✍️ Section 3 — Justification block (3 points)

The part a human reads closely. Roughly a paragraph each, with numbers from your own cells.

Item Points What earns them
3.1 1 The specification, before the machine. \(D\), \(d\) and the log decision, each tied to a test or a plot. The lags at the start of the ACF/PACF that named \(p, q\), and the seasonal lags that named \(P, Q\).
3.2 1 What AutoARIMA chose, and what you submitted. Where it agrees or disagrees with your reading, and why. Your candidates with AICc and rolling MASE. The model you submitted and the evidence for it — and if it is not AutoARIMA’s choice, what made you leave it.
3.3 1 What is left, and what you expect. Ljung-Box with \(p + q + P + Q\) degrees of freedom, read. The residual autocorrelation at lag \(m\), against your Lab 6 model. The interval’s coverage on the validation window. Your expected skill — and if you expect to lose, why.
Important

Marked on the reasoning. A hand reading that AutoARIMA overrules, with a clear account of why, scores full marks in 3.1 and 3.2. So does a model that loses to the benchmark, if 3.3 explains the loss with numbers.

WarningWhere this goes wrong
  • Reading only the first lags. \(P\) and \(Q\) live at \(m, 2m, 3m\) — the shaded lags on the template’s plot. A spike at lag 12 with nothing at 24 is a seasonal MA(1); a decay over 12, 24, 36 is seasonal AR.
  • Two seasonal differences. \(D \le 1\), always, and \(d + D \le 2\).
  • Comparing AICc across different \(d\), \(D\) or log choices. Across those, rolling_mase decides.
  • Ljung-Box with \(p + q\) only. The seasonal terms spend degrees of freedom too: \(p + q + P + Q\).
  • Freezing AutoARIMA’s orders from train. If you choose AutoARIMA, submit it refitted on all of y — that is the model the pool guarantee is about.
  • Forecasting from train. The submission is refitted on the whole history.
