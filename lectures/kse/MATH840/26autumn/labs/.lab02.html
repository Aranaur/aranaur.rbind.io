You did all of this in Week 1. It should take ten minutes.

Warning⚠️ If your series ends with missing values

Some series in the pool stop being reported years before their last row: the file ends with a long run of blanks. interpolate() will happily fill those by repeating the last value, and then your holdout is a flat line you invented — every method “predicts” it perfectly and the exercise is meaningless.

Trim to the last real observation before you split, and say in your report that you did and how much you dropped. Filling interior gaps is repair; filling the tail is fabrication.

Warning⚠️ Where this goes wrong: the frequency string
  • "W" does not mean “weekly”. It means week ending Sunday. If your timestamps are Mondays, a grid built with "W" lines up with nothing and your series comes back empty. Use the anchored form, "W-MON" — which is what spec["freq"] already gives you.
  • pandas 3 removed the bare "M", "Q", "Y" and "H" aliases. Use "MS"/"ME", "QS"/"QE", "YS"/"YE" and lowercase "h". A bare "Q" now raises rather than warns.
  • "MS" is month start, "ME" is month end. Pick the one your timestamps already use, or every row misses the grid by a few days.

After building a grid, check you did not lose everything: s["y"].notna().sum() should be close to the number of rows you started with.

🔢 Section 3 — Implementation (2 points)

The four methods. Implement them yourself — they are four lines each, and they are on the Week 1 slides if you need them.

\[ \hat{y}_{T+h} = \bar{y} \qquad \hat{y}_{T+h} = y_T \qquad \hat{y}_{T+h} = y_{T+h-m} \qquad \hat{y}_{T+h} = y_T + h\,\frac{y_T - y_1}{T-1} \]

The holdout. Cut off the last full seasonal cycle — one year of data — fit nothing, and forecast that span with all four. Report the RMSE of each, and the ratio of each to the best. That ratio is the skill score from the lecture: it says how much worse a method is than the bar.

The verdict. Which won? Did your prediction hold? If it did not, say what in the plots you misread — that sentence is worth more than the prediction was.

Warning⚠️ Where this goes wrong
  • snaive needs the last m observations of the training part, not of the whole series. If your forecast looks suspiciously perfect, you are forecasting with the answer.
  • Drop or interpolate missing values before scoring: an NaN anywhere in the holdout makes every RMSE NaN.
  • RMSE is in the units of your series, so it is only comparable between methods on the same series — never between your series and a classmate’s. That is what the ratio is for.
