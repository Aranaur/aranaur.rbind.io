Warning⚠️ Where this goes wrong: the frequency string
  • "W" does not mean “weekly”. It means week ending Sunday. If your timestamps are Mondays, a grid built with "W" lines up with nothing and your series comes back empty. Use the anchored form, "W-MON" — which is what spec["freq"] already gives you.
  • pandas 3 removed the bare "M", "Q", "Y" and "H" aliases. Use "MS"/"ME", "QS"/"QE", "YS"/"YE" and lowercase "h". A bare "Q" now raises rather than warns.
  • "MS" is month start, "ME" is month end. Pick the one your timestamps already use, or every row misses the grid by a few days.

After building a grid, check that you did not lose everything: s["y"].notna().sum() should be close to the number of rows you started with.

Variance. Does the size of the seasonal swing grow with the level of the series? If it does, the additive decompositions below are the wrong model for it.

  • Estimate a Box-Cox \(\lambda\) (boxcox_lambda from coreforecast, as in the lecture).
  • Plot the transformed series next to the original.
  • Decide whether to work transformed or untransformed, and justify the decision in a sentence. A defensible “no transformation needed” is worth full marks; an unexplained transformation is not.

📖 boxcox / boxcox_lambda · scipy.stats.boxcox · FPP 3.1 transformations · FPP the Pythonic Way, ch. 3

WarningIf your series contains zeros

boxcox_lambda(..., method="loglik") refuses non-positive values outright. method="guerrero" accepts them but may return \(\lambda = 0\), which is the log transform — undefined at zero. A multiplicative decomposition refuses them for the same reason.

That is information about your series, not an obstacle. Find out where the zeros come from before deciding what to do about them, and say what you concluded. This is exactly the kind of judgement the point is for.

Decomposition. Two of them, on whichever version of the series you settled on:

  • classical decomposition (seasonal_decompose);
  • STL (STL), with a stated choice of seasonal and robust.

Then compare them. Where do they disagree, and which do you trust more on this series?

Warning⚠️ Where this goes wrong
  • seasonal_decompose returns NaN for the first and last period / 2 observations — six months at each end of a monthly series. That is the centred moving average, not a bug, and it is one of the differences you are asked to comment on.
  • model="multiplicative" refuses zeros and negative values outright.
  • STL(...) needs seasonal to be an odd integer ≥ 3, and it must be an int, not a float. robust=True costs a little accuracy and buys resistance to outliers.
  • Both need period — the number of observations in one cycle, which is mine["season"], not the number of years.

🔢 Section 3 — Implementation (2 points)

Strength of components. Compute, from the STL fit:

\[ F_T = \max\left(0,\; 1 - \frac{\operatorname{Var}(R_t)}{\operatorname{Var}(T_t + R_t)}\right) \qquad F_S = \max\left(0,\; 1 - \frac{\operatorname{Var}(R_t)}{\operatorname{Var}(S_t + R_t)}\right) \]

Report both, rounded to two decimals.

📖 FPP 4.3 — where \(F_T\) and \(F_S\) come from

Both come out of a fitted STL object: fit.trend, fit.seasonal, fit.resid. If you get a value outside \([0, 1]\), you have almost certainly passed the STL object instead of the result of .fit().
