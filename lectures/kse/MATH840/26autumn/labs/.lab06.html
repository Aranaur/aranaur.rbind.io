📊 Section 1 — EDA and visualisation (1 point)

You saw this series in Week 4. This time the plots have one job: to justify each letter of your specification before AutoETS runs.

  • A time plot, read for trend: is there one, and does it flatten (damping)?
  • The seasonal swing against the level — one point per year, max minus min of the year against its mean. A flat cloud says additive season; a cloud that climbs with the level says multiplicative. The template gives you swing_against_level().
  • The validation split on a plot: history, validation window, your chosen model’s forecast and 80% interval over it.
  • One sentence per plot. A plot with no reading attached earns nothing.

🎯 Section 2 — Forecast accuracy (3 points)

Scored from your submission file, on the h observations after your history that nobody has seen.

\[ \text{skill} = \frac{\text{MASE of your forecast}}{\text{MASE of your benchmark}} \]

Skill Points
below 1 — you beat your benchmark 3
1 to 1.1 — within 10% 2
above 1.1 1, and 3.3 must say why
  • Your benchmark is the best of the four simple methods — mean, naive, snaive, drift — by RMSE on the last h observations of your history, refitted on all of it. The template computes it by exactly the scorer’s rule, so the number you see is the one you are compared against.
  • MASE is scaled by the in-sample seasonal naive error of your whole history — the same mase() as in the template.
  • You are never compared with your classmates.
NoteWhat the pool guarantees, and what it does not

Every series in the pool was issued only if AutoETS fitted on the whole history beats its benchmark on the hidden horizon. That is a guarantee about AutoETS rerun on all of y — not about every ETS model, and not about the letters AutoETS picked on train.

The Week 4 lecture’s rule — shortlist on AICc, decide on a rolling origin — is still the right way to work, and it is what 3.2 pays for. Measured on the whole pool: shortlisting the three best models by AICc and choosing among them by a rolling origin beats the benchmark on 94% of series and comes within 10% on 98%; choosing by a rolling origin among everything, 85%. If your evidence leads you away from AutoETS, follow it — and say in 3.2 what that evidence was.

✍️ Section 3 — Justification block (3 points)

The part a human reads closely. Roughly a paragraph each, with numbers from your own cells.

Item Points What earns them

3.2 1 What AutoETS chose, and what you submitted. Where AutoETS agrees or disagrees with you, and why. Your shortlist with AICc and rolling MASE. The model you submitted and the evidence for it — and if it is not AutoETS’s choice, what made you leave it.
3.3 1 What is left, and what you expect. ACF and Ljung-Box on the innovation residuals, read: what structure is left and what it damages — the forecast or the interval. Your interval’s coverage on the validation window. Your expected skill on the hidden horizon, and if you expect to lose, why.
Important

Marked on the reasoning. A specification by hand that AutoETS overrules, with a clear account of why, scores full marks in 3.1 and 3.2. A model that wins and cannot say why it was chosen does not.

WarningWhere this goes wrong
  • Specifying after the fact. MY_SPEC copied from AutoETS and justified backwards reads exactly like that. Write 3.1 from Section 1 alone.
  • Choosing on one window. The validation window is one draw of h observations. In the practice session it ranked a damped model first; over three windows the same model was last of the shortlist.
  • Comparing AICc across transformations. AICc is comparable between ETS models on the same y; not between y and log(y), and not against anything that is not ETS.
  • damped=None. With model="AAA" and no damped, AutoETS may return ETS(A,Ad,A). The template’s fit_ets() passes damped explicitly — use it.
  • Testing the wrong residuals. Ljung-Box goes on fit.model_["residuals"] of the chosen model, not on y.
  • Forecasting from train. The submission is refitted on the whole history.
  • Freezing AutoETS’s letters from train. If you choose AutoETS, submit fit_ets(y) — the search rerun on all of y. On half the pool it lands on different letters than it did on train, and the guarantee is about the rerun, not about the letters it found first.
